I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in AI Security and Privacy Auditing at the University of York. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, security and privacy — building tools and methods to audit existing and future AI systems for the harms they can cause.

I hold a PhD in Machine Learning Techniques for Communication Networks from Loughborough University, where my thesis critically evaluated Federated Learning and Split Learning under adversarial poisoning attacks. Along the way I developed Delphi, a novel poisoning attack that induces uncertainty using Bayesian optimisation, reinforcement learning and multi-objective methods, backed by a graph-theoretic analysis of vulnerabilities in distributed learning.

Current Work

I work on the Participatory Harm Auditing Workbenches and Methodologies (PHAWM) project, developing auditing tools for security- and privacy-related issues in AI algorithms. This includes:

  • Privacy games and attacks against foundation models
  • Reinforcement learning algorithms for alignment
  • Synthetic data generation for auditing and evaluation
