My research develops methods to make AI systems auditable, secure and privacy-preserving — with a particular focus on distributed and federated settings.

Research Interests

Privacy

Understanding and mitigating privacy leakage in machine learning, including membership inference and privacy risks in foundation models.

Privacy Games

Game-theoretic formulations of attacks and defences, framing privacy and security as adversarial interactions to be measured and audited.

AI Auditing

Participatory harm auditing workbenches and methodologies (PHAWM) for evaluating the security and privacy behaviour of existing and future AI algorithms.

Federated Learning

Robustness of federated and split learning under adversarial poisoning, and the trustworthiness of distributed and personalised learning systems.

PhD Research

In my PhD, Critical Evaluation of Federated Learning and Split Learning using Adversarial Poisoning Attacks, I developed Delphi — a novel poisoning attack that induces uncertainty via four methods: Bayesian optimisation, least squares, reinforcement learning and multi-objective Bayesian optimisation. I complemented this with a graph-theoretic analysis of the vulnerabilities inherent in distributed learning.

Methods & Tools

Adversarial machine learning, reinforcement learning, Bayesian and convex optimisation, graph analysis, federated / split / personalised federated learning, and multi-agent reinforcement learning — applied across security, IoT and communication-network settings.
